§01 — The gap

The cuff can’t read a stiff artery.

The ankle-brachial index is the default screen for peripheral artery disease: two blood pressures, one ratio. It’s fast and cheap. But in patients with diabetes or kidney disease, calcified arteries resist the cuff, the ankle reads falsely high, and disease can hide behind a normal-looking number.2

6.5M+ U.S. adults aged 40 and older are living with peripheral artery disease.1 Many don’t know it.
ABI > 1.40
Arteries too stiff to compress. Guidelines treat the reading as non-diagnostic.2
False reassurance
A normal-looking ratio in a high-risk patient can delay the next test.
Late detection
Found later, vascular disease is associated with worse outcomes and heavier treatment.
§02 — Waveforms

Pressure is a number. Flow has a form.

As disease narrows an artery, the Doppler waveform downstream changes shape in a recognizable progression. Calcification doesn’t distort that shape the way it distorts cuff pressure. That progression is the signal ImageAiD is designed around.

§03 — Method

Three steps, one visit.

  1. i.

    Scan

    A handheld probe captures tibial waveforms in minutes at the point of care. No cuff, no occlusion.

  2. ii.

    Analyze

    Software-supported interpretation of waveform shape, intended to aid clinical assessment.

  3. iii.

    Act

    Structured output designed to slot into the clinical workflow and follow-up decisions.
