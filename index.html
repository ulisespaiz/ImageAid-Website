The gap

The standard screen has blind spots.

The ankle-brachial index is quick and cheap. For some of the patients who need it most, it's also the least reliable.

ABI can miss high-risk patients

In people with diabetes or chronic kidney disease, ABI alone can return false negatives, which can delay further workup.

Calcified arteries skew pressure

Medial arterial calcification makes vessels noncompressible. The cuff reads high, and a pressure ratio stops meaning much.

Late detection raises the stakes

When vascular disease is found later, it's associated with worse outcomes and a heavier treatment burden.

Sources: CDC, About PAD; Diabetol Metab Syndr 2024, TBI vs ABI in type 2 diabetes.

Our approach

Pressure gives you a number. A waveform shows you the shape of flow.

Doppler waveform shape changes as arterial disease progresses, and calcification doesn't distort it the way it distorts cuff pressure. ImageAiD's platform is being built to capture that signal at the tibial arteries and return structured output a clinician can act on.

Triphasic

Typical normal

Sharp upstroke, brief reverse flow, then a small forward wave.

Biphasic

Changing

Forward and reverse phases remain, but the late forward wave is lost.

Monophasic

Dampened

Slow rise, rounded peak, no reversal. Often seen beyond a significant obstruction.

Waveforms are simplified illustrations for education, not output from the ImageAiD platform.
